## Experiment

The experiment used 185 held-out essays from one ELLIPSE writing task. It
compared three independent estimates of essay quality: the transparent scorer,
the hybrid scorer, and a comparative-judgment ranking produced from pairwise
essay comparisons.

The comparative-judgment results were added only after the two scorers had
produced their estimates. They were not used to train either scorer.

Quadratic weighted kappa (QWK) measures agreement with the human scores. It
ranges from 0 for chance-level agreement to 1 for perfect agreement. A higher
value means that the estimates agree more closely with the human scores.

## Results

Adding the comparative-judgment ranking improved both scorers on the same 185
essays.

| Scorer | Scorer alone | With comparative judgment | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Hybrid | 0.739 | 0.762 | +0.023 |
| Transparent | 0.727 | 0.757 | +0.030 |

A second comparison evaluated each estimate separately and then averaged all
three with equal weight.

| Estimate | QWK |
| --- | ---: |
| Transparent scorer | 0.727 |
| Hybrid scorer | 0.739 |
| Comparative judgment | 0.754 |
| Equal average of all three | 0.766 |

The equal average also had the smallest mean absolute error, 0.257 scale
points, compared with 0.276 for the transparent scorer, 0.281 for the hybrid
scorer, and 0.287 for comparative judgment.

## Interpretation

The comparative-judgment ranking contained information that was not already
present in either scorer. Combining the three estimates agreed with the human
scores more closely than any estimate on its own.

This result concerns one writing task and 185 essays. It requires replication
across writing tasks before it can support a general claim about the
instrument.
